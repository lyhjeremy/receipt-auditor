Receipt Auditor
fine-tuning series / LoRA vs Claude

Local LoRA fine-tune, benchmarked against Claude

A 1.5B model fine-tuned on a laptop sorts receipt items at 90.5%. Claude zero-shot: 80.0%.

Receipt Auditor reads photographed receipts, checks each total in code, strips card numbers and other personal data, and puts every line item in one of 12 spend categories. The categorizer is Qwen2.5-1.5B with a LoRA adapter trained on 4,799 synthetic line items. Claude, given the 12 labels and asked for one, gets 80.0% on the same rows. Most of its misses are items whose generated labels a person would argue with, which the fine-tune learned and Claude could not. Section 02 goes through them.

Code and benchmark Engineering notes

Benchmark

21 errors in 220, most of them between neighbors

The training data is 4,799 synthetic line items generated with claude -p in the abbreviated styles point-of-sale systems print: SBUX #4821, WM SUPERCENTER #2847, TST* JOE'S PIZZA. It is split by merchant into 4,309 train, 270 validation and 220 test rows, and a leakage check confirmed no merchant sits on both sides. Training ran on Apple Silicon with MLX: batch 4, 12 LoRA layers, 800 iterations, learning rate 1.5e-4. Validation loss fell from 5.085 to 0.419.

The fine-tuned model gets 199 of the 220 test items right. Its mistakes cluster where the categories touch: household goods called other, travel called transport, transport called fuel.

Reading the gap

Most of the 10.5-point gap is label noise

Claude misses 44 of 220. 38 of those are rows the generator labeled other or transport where Claude answered household, health or fuel: paint, screws and HDMI cables labeled other, gasoline labeled transport. It gets none of the 28 other rows. The LoRA figure stands; these are the things to keep in mind when comparing it with Claude's.

  1. The labels are noisy, and the fine-tune learned the noise

    The test labels come from the same synthetic generator as the training data. A first-aid kit at CVS is other; Shell unleaded is fuel on one row and transport on another. The adapter learned those habits. Claude, answering one item at a time, often gives the more defensible category and is marked wrong.

  2. Claude's prompt lists the labels; the fine-tune's does not

    Claude got the training prompt plus the 12 allowed labels and an instruction to answer with one of them. A zero-shot model has no other way to know the label set; the adapter learned it in training. The prompt is in eval/claude_rerun/prompt_template.txt.

  3. The base model's score comes from a reconstructed scorer

    The script that produced eval/benchmark.md was lost before it was committed. training/bench_categorizer.py was rebuilt against the stored raw outputs. It reproduces the test-set order and the LoRA figure exactly, because the adapter's outputs are bare category names. For the base model, which answered in prose, it gets about 26% against the published 27.7%.

Rerun, 2026-10-28. Until this date the page reported Claude at 56.0% on 150 rows. That run used claude -p from inside the repo with file access, and 101 of its 150 answers cite src/schemas.py, so most had read the category list from the code. Its answers were prose, 83 naming more than one category, and the script that picked one is lost. The rerun copies only the test rows and the prompt into a scratch directory, runs the CLI there with every tool disabled, and scores with an exact-match rule that lists anything else as invalid. Runner, prompt, raw outputs, scorer and results are in eval/claude_rerun/.

Everything above is on synthetic data. The spec calls for 10 to 20 hand-labeled receipts of my own as a final test, and the dataset card still records zero.

What the result does support is narrower than the headline. A 1.5B model with a 4M-parameter adapter learns a fixed 12-label scheme, quirks included, well enough to replace a frontier-model call for this step at no per-call cost.

Design

The model phrases numbers; code computes them

Two language models touch a receipt, and neither does arithmetic. Each step below is labeled with what runs it.

  1. 01codetesseract reads the photo locally.
  2. 02claude -pOne call per receipt rejects photos that are not receipts and fills a Pydantic Receipt. The raw OCR text is then discarded.
  3. 03codeprivacy.sweep_receipt masks card-like digit runs to the last four and replaces emails and phone numbers.
  4. 04codereconcile adds line items and tax and compares the sum with the printed total. A mismatch is flagged for a one-tap correction, never adjusted to agree.
  5. 05LoRA 1.5BEach line item gets one of 12 categories.
  6. Later, a spending question
  7. 06pandasanalysis.answer_query computes the figure from the stored receipts.
  8. 07claude -pWrites one sentence around the computed numbers.
  9. 08codeassert_numbers_present checks every number survived to within a cent. If one was dropped or changed, the app shows a template sentence instead.

Privacy

Four layers of PII handling

The Receipt and LineItem schemas have no field for a cardholder name, card number, address or loyalty number, so extraction never asks for them. The raw OCR text is discarded once a validated Receipt exists. The regex sweep in step 03 catches what slips through and reports how many redactions it made. On a hosted deployment, receipts stay in memory for the session; privacy.is_space_environment() is the one check both paths use before writing anything to disk.

Build

Stack and series

Classifier
Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct (4-bit), LoRA via MLX on Apple Silicon, 3.96M trainable parameters, served with mlx_lm.generate
Extraction
tesseract OCR, then one claude -p call per receipt
Validation
Pydantic schemas; reconciliation and narration guard in src/reconcile.py
Queries
Spoken or typed questions parsed into a structured Query, answered by pandas, repeats served from a semantic cache
App
Gradio: batch upload, reconciled grid, dashboard, voice query
Tests
19, across reconciliation, PII redaction and the spend-query layer
Series
One of four apps that fine-tune a local model and benchmark it against Claude: Cellar Scanner, Race Day Copilot, Receipt Auditor, Menu Decoder
